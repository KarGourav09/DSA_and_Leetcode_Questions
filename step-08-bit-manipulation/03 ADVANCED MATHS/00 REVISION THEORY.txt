BIT AND NUMBER THEORY - REVISION THEORY

Bit manipulation often turns arithmetic structure into constant-time operations, but bounds and integer representation still matter.

Prime counting
The Sieve of Eratosthenes marks composite multiples beginning at p*p. It counts primes through N in O(N log log N) time and O(N) memory. Use a wide type for p*p.

Divisors
Divisors occur in pairs d and n/d. Iterate d through sqrt(n), add both members, and avoid adding the square root twice. Sorting is separate from enumeration.

Two unique numbers
XOR every input to obtain a^b. Choose the lowest set bit of that XOR to partition values into two groups; each duplicate remains in one group and the two unique values separate.

Bit-width discipline
The expression 1<<i may overflow or use signed arithmetic; use 1u or a wider unsigned type when appropriate. Do not shift by a negative amount or by a value equal to the type width. State whether numbers are non-negative.

Revision questions: What is the numeric bound? Can multiplication overflow? Is the algorithm using a bit identity valid for signed values? Is output order relevant?
