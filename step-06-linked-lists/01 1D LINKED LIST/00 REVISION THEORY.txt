SINGLY LINKED LIST - REVISION THEORY

A singly linked list stores a value and a pointer to the next node. The head identifies the first node; a null next pointer marks the end. Nodes are not contiguous, so indexing is O(n), while insertion or deletion is O(1) when the correct node or predecessor is already known.

Traversal
Start at head and repeatedly move current=current->next until null. Never dereference a null pointer. Keep a separate head update when an operation changes the first node.

Insertion and deletion
Insert at head by making the new node point to head, then returning the new node. Insert at tail by tracking the last node. For deleting the head, save head->next and release the old node. For deleting a kth node or value X, track the predecessor and reconnect predecessor->next to target->next.

Invariants
At every step, processed nodes remain reachable in their original order. A deletion must preserve the suffix and must not lose the successor before changing links.

Edge cases: empty list, one node, deleting the head/tail, missing value, duplicate values, and k outside the list. In C++, delete allocated nodes when ownership ends.
